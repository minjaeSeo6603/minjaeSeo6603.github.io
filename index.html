Data analysis · Causal inference · Credit & household finance

What the data can — and cannot — identify.

I am a Master of Arts in Public Policy candidate at the University of Chicago Harris School, with a Certificate in Research Methods and a B.A. in Statistics from UC Berkeley. My work sits at the boundary of what an identification strategy actually delivers: where a design point-identifies an effect, where it only bounds one, and how much of a reported result survives when the assumptions behind it are relaxed.

That question is the same one an expert report, a credit model, or a product experiment has to answer, so I build the whole path to it: cleaning and merging the raw files, choosing an estimator that survives its own diagnostics, and shipping Python, R, and Stata workflows another analyst can rerun end to end and get the same numbers.

02 Research papers Four

Robustness of Treatment Effects between Common and Heterogeneous Treatment Effects

Working paper · July 2026

A randomized experiment identifies the two marginal distributions of the potential outcomes, but nothing about their joint law. By Sklar's theorem the dependence between them lives in a copula that the data leave completely unrestricted — and every object that describes heterogeneity is a functional of that unidentified joint: the distribution of individual effects, its dispersion, the fraction made worse off, rank preservation itself. Assuming a common effect is therefore not an innocuous simplification. It is the selection of one copula out of a class the experiment cannot distinguish. This paper declines that selection and reports the identified set instead.

A single proposition then yields three regimes, according to what the marginals alone permit. When they are identical and non-degenerate, homogeneity and maximal heterogeneity are observationally equivalent and the experiment cannot separate them. When they are not a location shift, heterogeneity is forced: a program can raise average earnings while the Fréchet floor guarantees a strictly positive mass of people made worse off. When a marginal is degenerate, the Fréchet class collapses to a single point and a common effect becomes identified rather than assumed — identification bought by ceilings and floors, not by any assumption about dependence.

Design
Partial identification — sharp bounds, no point estimate
Tools
Sklar's theorem · Fréchet–Hoeffding · Makarov bounds
Result
One proposition, three regimes: undetermined, forced, or collapsed

Impact of AI Exposure on Labor Demand: Firm-Size Heterogeneity

Working paper · May 2026

The November 2022 release of ChatGPT was the first general-purpose deployment of a language model able to substitute for cognitive labor at scale. This paper asks whether that shock reshaped hiring, and whether the response was symmetric across the firm-size distribution. The triple-difference design exploits three margins: occupation-level LLM exposure, within-industry firm size, and the pre/post-ChatGPT split, on a 14.5-million-cell panel of U.S. public-firm job postings from Revelio Labs merged to Compustat fundamentals, 2018–2025.

Within-size coefficients are sharply negative at both small (−0.211, SE 0.033) and large (−0.146, SE 0.026) firms. The triple interaction runs against the prior that large firms lead in IT adoption, but the interval covers zero and the design cannot yet reject a null. Next steps extend the outcome to separation rates and WARN-Act layoffs, add PPML as a robustness check on the log(· + 1) specification, and test alternative exposure measures and treatment dates.

Design
Triple difference (DDD)
Data
Revelio Labs postings · Compustat · Eloundou LLM exposure
Panel

Channels of Negative Earnings for Community-College Transfer Students: A Selection-and-Mediation Analysis in NLSY97

Working paper · May 2026

Two-to-four-year transfer students earn less than direct-entry BA holders, but the size of that gap and the channel behind it are both contested. Using the NLSY97 cohort, this paper estimates the transfer wage penalty conditional on BA completion under a Heckman-style selection-on-observables design augmented with the 1997 Achenbach Behavior Problems Index, then tests post-treatment mental health as a mediator under both Baron–Kenny and ACME frameworks.

Two findings carry the paper. The IPW estimate lands close to the −$7,000 administrative benchmark in Miller (2026). And a previously reported non-cognitive control effect turns out to be a sample-composition artifact: holding the sample fixed at N = 740 cuts the Achenbach attenuation from 27.5% to 4.5%. The mediated effect through mental health is a precise null at the available sample size.

Selection on observables · PSM · IPW · AIPW · ACME mediation

Intergenerational Income Mobility and Private Education in South Korea, 1998–2024

Working paper · April 2026

A cohort analysis of income persistence across the 1960–2000 birth cohorts, built on waves 1–27 of the Korean Labor and Income Panel Study and a rigorously deduplicated sample of 5,388 children, one observation each. The paper is as much about lifecycle bias as about mobility: the apparent decline in persistence across cohorts is contaminated at both ends, upward for early cohorts observed at parental ages 55–65 and downward for late cohorts observed at 25–30, so the trend is read as suggestive rather than structural.

Daughters show roughly twice the elasticity of sons — a margin the existing Korean literature, focused on sons, has passed over. Quantile regression puts persistence highest at the bottom of the distribution (0.161 at the 10th percentile against 0.057 at the 90th), and the regional ordering runs Capital Region > Metropolitan > Province.

Step 2 asks why those regional gradients exist and how regional private education markets and CSAT competition sustain them. Subtracting initial log income from both sides of the IGE equation yields a growth-rate form algebraically equivalent to the cross-regional regression of Durlauf, Kourtellos and Minkin (2001); their varying-coefficient strategy is adopted with regional average CSAT scores as the smoothing variable. Inference uses wild cluster bootstrap throughout, given only J = 12–17 regional clusters.

Design
IGE · rank–rank · quantile regression · varying-coefficient IGE
Data
KLIPS waves 1–27 (1998–2024) · N = 5,388
Inference
Wild cluster bootstrap (Cameron, Gelbach & Miller 2008)

Step 1 — long-run trends Step 2 — regional heterogeneity
